Three questions we keep coming back to (every morning, before coffee)

Good skiing starts with good water. Our researchers apply rigorous observational methods, mainly standing on the dock and pointing, to identify the conditions that turn a lake from choppy and crowded into calm, clear and skiable. Findings are delivered to skiers before they leave the dock, or shouted at them afterward.

  • How flat is the water?

    Surface disturbance (known in the literature as “chop”) increases throughout the day as a function of wind speed, boat traffic, and the number of people who said they'd be up at 6 a.m. but weren't. Hourly observations consistently show peak flatness just after sunrise, a finding we've replicated every summer and that everyone who sleeps in continues to ignore.

  • How warm is the water?

    Water temperature governs several key variables, including wetsuit selection, time spent on the dock deciding, and the volume of the noise a researcher makes when falling in. We measure temperature by season and by depth, depth being however far the researcher was willing to go.

  • Are there other boats out?

    Boat density is inversely correlated with ski quality and directly correlated with people waving at you for no reason. We track traffic by time of day and season so skiers can find open water, and so we can finally identify who keeps making wake at 7 a.m.
