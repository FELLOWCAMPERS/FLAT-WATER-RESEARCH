Still water shows what moves beneath it.

Flatwater Research Institute studies how lakes, rivers and wetlands change through the seasons, and shares what we learn with the people who depend on them.

Explore our research Work with us

Three questions we keep coming back to

Our work follows water from snowpack to shoreline, and asks what its condition tells us about the land and climate around it.

  • How do lakes layer and mix?

    Summer heat and winter ice divide a lake into layers. We track when those layers form and break down, and what that means for oxygen, nutrients and fish habitat.

  • Where does the water come from?

    We follow rain and snowmelt from headwaters to shore to see how land use, drought and flooding change what flows into a watershed.

  • When is water safe to use?

    We watch for algae blooms, contaminants and low-oxygen events, and publish clear readings that communities and regulators can act on.

How we work

We measure in place.

Instruments in the water and people on the shore record conditions over years, not just field seasons.

We publish what we find.

Data, methods and code are shared openly so other researchers and local groups can check and reuse them.

We work with the people nearby.

Our questions come from landowners, Indigenous communities, lake associations and municipal staff who see the water every day.
