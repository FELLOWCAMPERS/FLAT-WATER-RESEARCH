Three questions we keep coming back to

Good skiing starts with good water. We track the conditions that turn a lake from choppy and crowded into calm, clear and skiable, and put that information in skiers' hands before they ever leave the dock.

  • How flat is the water?

    Wake and chop build through the day as wind rises and boat traffic increases. We track surface conditions hour by hour to find the calmest windows, usually right after sunrise, when a lake is closest to glass.

  • How warm is the water?

    Water temperature shapes everything from what to wear to how a fall feels. We monitor temperature through the season and by depth, so skiers know what to expect before they're in it.

  • Are there other boats out?

    A crowded lake changes everything: wake interference, right-of-way, safety margins. We track typical traffic by time of day and season to help skiers find open water.

How we work

We analyze daily conditions. (very exhaustive work)

Buoys in the water and people on the shore record conditions over full seasons, not just a handful of mornings.

We do our best to keep the public informed.

Conditions data and methods are shared openly so skiers, clubs and marinas can check it and put it to use.

We work with the people on the water. (very important)

Our questions come from skiers, boat clubs, marinas and lake associations who are out there watching conditions every day.

Work with us

Questions, comments and ideas for working together are all welcome. We collaborate with ski clubs, marinas, lake associations and universities, and we welcome researchers studying water sports and lake conditions.
